In respondents' own experiences

Early qualitative themes

4 open-ended contributors
01

Loss can reveal the value of connection

“Friendship is important and fragile. Value your friends!”

A respondent reflecting on the death of a close friend.

02

Competence can become tied to self-worth

“It's really hard for me to stop striving.”

A respondent describing anxiety connected to feeling a need to be capable, reliable, and needed.

03

Perfectionism may connect different miseries

“I try hard to be perfect even though I know that is not rational. I do not give myself slack.”

The same respondent linked perfectionism and limited self-forgiveness to guilt.

04

Waiting can crowd out the present

“The misery of waiting is something I know inherently is preventing me from enjoying the present, but it is difficult to escape.”

A respondent describing health-related uncertainty and waiting for a procedure.

05

Uncertainty can create a search for reassurance

“You just want any little story about how someone in your situation had things work out positively, even though you know the stats and know it's a long shot.”

A respondent describing a prolonged period of uncertainty while hoping for a positive outcome.

06

Hope can make negative thoughts feel guilty

“There is a little guilt if you let yourself think about things not working out well.”

The same respondent described guilt arising alongside hope and dread during a difficult wait.

These themes summarize a very small number of responses and are offered as observations, not general conclusions.
